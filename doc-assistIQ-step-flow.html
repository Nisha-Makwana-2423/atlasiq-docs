Before anything starts: set-up and old data

Screens: S31 S32 S33 S05 · 15 requirements

What happens. This happens once per customer, before go-live or when a contract changes. The team loads old records with their original received dates, gives people access, and sets up each service type's required-document lists (one for the original request, one for the final request, each with dated versions), the letter templates and inserts, the review clocks and the holiday calendar. Example. 1

Initial Notice at placement, then a records request for every weekly batch

Screens: S12 S11 · 10 requirements

What happens. This happens at two moments. When OCPI places a provider on prepay review, the Letters Team sends the Initial Notice once. It goes by USPS traceable mail and is also posted on the portal, at least 20 calendar days before NCTracks starts holding claims. After that, NCTracks sends claims weekly. For every batch, an Original Records Request is posted on the portal within 48 hours of the batch date. It lists the DLIs under review and encloses the service type's document list. Records requests go to the portal only.

Example. OCPI's memo places Sunrise on review on 20 August 2026. The Initial Notice is mailed by certified mail and posted that day, so claims can be held from 9 September. On Monday 21 September the first batch arrives with claim 88231. The Original Records Request is posted on Tuesday 22 September.

2

Letters are tracked and the records due date is worked out

Screens: S12 S03 · 8 requirements

What happens. The system tracks every letter: each USPS event for mailed letters, and posting, email and pick-up for portal letters. Records are due 5 business days after the request; weekends and holidays never count against the provider. If a provider does not pick up a portal letter within 5 business days, it is marked "not picked up" and the batch moves on. CQH's own deadlines run in calendar days and start from the due date.

Example. The request posted Tuesday 22 September is due Tuesday 29 September. CQH's clocks start from there: final request by 14 October (day 15), review of complete DLIs by 19 October (day 20). Around Thanksgiving the same rule gives the provider longer: a request on Monday 23 November is due Wednesday 2 December.

3

Provider sends the records

Screens: S10 · 6 requirements

What happens. Providers send records by portal upload or by fax. On the portal they choose the agency and NPI and add many files at once; they do not upload claim by claim, and CQH matches documents to DLIs while indexing. One submission covers one NPI only. Before the first claims, the provider must also send a master list of staff names and aliases (plus any acronym, EVV code or DME catalogue lists). Fax is often the main channel: in one NC case, 595 of 775 deliveries were faxes. Faxes already held by the old fax system are read in too.

Example. Sunrise's billing clerk signs in, picks Sunrise Home Care and its NPI, and uploads 6 PDFs for the 21 September batch: aide service notes, Sara's training records, the plan of care and the service authorization. A smaller agency faxes its pages with a cover sheet.

4

The system collects and stamps everything that arrives

Screens: S10 S17 · 5 requirements

What happens. Loading runs by itself several times a day and collects files from the portal, the fax server, FTP and scans. For portal uploads, provider and NPI come from the agency the provider chose; for other sources they are read from the file name or the fax cover sheet. Pages are counted, and every page is stamped with the date and time to the second and the delivery method. If fewer pages arrive than expected, the gap is found.

Example. Sunrise's upload lands at 10:42:08 on Monday 28 September. The system takes provider and NPI from the upload, counts 48 pages in 6 files, and stamps each page "28 Sep 2026 10:42:08, upload".

5

Receipt is confirmed, late items are flagged, the team is alerted

Screens: S10 · 5 requirements

7

Each document is indexed: the key facts are filled in

Screens: S13 · 7 requirements

What happens. Indexing records the key facts of each document so it can be matched to DLIs. The facts depend on the document type: a service note needs the date of service and staff name; a licence needs its effective and expiry dates. The system fills what it can and marks it machine-read, and a person checks it. Reading will not work on everything, because many notes are handwritten. If a staff name cannot be read, that DLI will need a final request. Staff names are checked against the provider's master staff list.

Example. Priya opens Mr. Rao's aide note for 10 September. Provider, patient, aide Sara and the visit time are filled in and marked machine-read. Sara's signature is handwritten, so Priya checks it against Sunrise's master staff list and confirms the name.
